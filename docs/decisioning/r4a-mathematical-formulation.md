# R4A bounded replenishment formulation

**Status:** CROSS-PLATFORM PASS on Actions run `30728662668`; mathematical contract for the bounded
synthetic R4A/L4 gate; full G4 OPEN

This document states the intended 14-day multi-line CP-SAT model in reviewer-readable notation.
The executable configuration and independent validator remain authoritative. No result in this
document is a claim of optimality, production value, or real-world calibration.

## 1. Sets and grain

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| \(i \in I\) | eligible SKU/location order lines |
| \(t \in \{1,\ldots,14\}\) | calendar day after the weekly planning cutoff |
| \(s \in S\) | `normal`, `demand_surge`, or `supplier_delay` |
| \(g \in G\) | suppliers |
| \(l \in L\) | locations |

Each line \(i\) maps to exactly one supplier \(g(i)\) and location \(l(i)\). Regular-order
quantities are first-stage decisions shared across scenarios. Inventory, fulfillment, lost sales,
and expedite are scenario recourse.

## 2. Registered inputs

The principal nonnegative inputs are stored as integer units and integer cents:

- \(D_{its}\): scenario demand;
- \(I^0_i\): opening on-hand inventory;
- \(\ell_{is}\): scenario regular-order lead time in days;
- \(m_i\), \(M_i\), and \(U_i\): order multiple, MOQ, and maximum order;
- \(B\): regular-order budget;
- \(C^{supplier}_g\): supplier capacity;
- \(C^{storage}_{lt}\): storage capacity;
- \(C^{expedite}_{lt}\): expedite capacity; and
- \(\rho\): configured minimum fill-rate target expressed by an exact integer ratio.

Volume, prices, and objective coefficients enter the bounded contract as integers.

Scenario objective weights \(w_s\) are frozen to one for every named scenario in R4A. Each unit
weight multiplies both the shared first-stage cost and that scenario's recourse cost. The separate
2000/6000/2000 configuration selects q10/q50/q90 marginal demand draws; neither set of weights is
claimed to be an empirical probability model.

## 3. Decision variables

| Variable | Domain | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| \(y_i\) | \(\{0,1\}\) | whether regular ordering is opened for line \(i\) |
| \(z_i\) | nonnegative integer | number of order multiples |
| \(q_i\) | nonnegative integer | regular-order quantity, \(q_i=m_i z_i\) |
| \(x_{its}\) | nonnegative integer | expedited quantity |
| \(b_{its}\) | nonnegative integer | lost sales |
| \(I_{its}\) | nonnegative integer | end-of-day inventory |

R4A models lost sales, not backlog. Transfer variables do not exist in this bounded formulation.

## 4. Regular-order constraints

For each line:

```text
q_i = m_i * z_i
M_i * y_i <= q_i <= U_i * y_i
```

When \(y_i=0\), the quantity is zero. When \(y_i=1\), the order respects MOQ, multiple, and the
line maximum. Ineligible lines are fixed to \(y_i=q_i=0\).

The shared budget and supplier constraints are:

```text
sum_i (fixed_cost_i * y_i + regular_unit_cost_i * q_i) <= B
sum_{i: g(i)=g} q_i <= supplier_capacity_g        for every supplier g
```

The budget includes fixed and unit purchase cost only. Working-capital cost is objective-only.

## 5. Lead-time-aware flow conservation

Let \(A_{its}\) be the arrival generated by the regular order:

```text
A_its = q_i  when t equals the scenario lead-time arrival day
A_its = 0    otherwise
```

With business days numbered \(t=1,\ldots,14\), an undelayed order with lead time \(\ell_i\)
arrives on business day \(t=\ell_i\). The implementation's zero-based array index is therefore
`lead_time_days - 1`; supplier delay adds its configured days before subtracting one.

Orders arriving outside the 14-day horizon contribute no within-horizon inventory. For every
line, day, and scenario:

Let \(V_{its}=I_{i,t-1,s}+A_{its}\) be regular inventory available before demand, and let
\(H_{its}\) be the same-day shortage:

```text
I_its = max(V_its - D_its, 0)
H_its = max(D_its - V_its, 0)
x_its + b_its = H_its
u_its = D_its - b_its
I_its >= 0, x_its >= 0, b_its >= 0
```

Expedite is same-day shortage recourse only. It can fulfill part of \(H_{its}\), but it cannot be
carried into ending inventory. Fulfilled demand \(u_{its}\) is derived rather than optimized as a
separate free variable.

For day 1, \(I_{i,0,s}=I^0_i\). These two equalities are the inventory and demand-conservation
identities. The independent validator recomputes them from persisted values.

## 6. Storage, expedite, and service constraints

For every location, day, and scenario:

```text
sum_{i: l(i)=l} volume_i * I_its <= storage_capacity_lt
sum_{i: l(i)=l} x_its <= expedite_capacity_lt
```

Both smoke locations use a hard expedite cap of 40 units per day. Expedite is therefore bounded
recourse: excess shortage becomes lost sales, making fill rate nontrivial.

The executable contract defines the exact service scope. Its integer form avoids floating-point
rounding. Conceptually, for every required scenario/scope:

```text
total fulfilled demand >= rho * total demand
```

Equivalently, lost sales may not exceed the configured fraction. Service is hard: an optimized or
fallback plan that fails it is independently infeasible.

## 7. Objective

The candidate minimizes a frozen integer-cent objective:

```text
weighted regular fixed order cost
+ weighted regular variable order cost
+ weighted scenario holding cost
+ weighted scenario stockout cost
+ weighted scenario expedite cost
+ weighted working-capital cost
```

In symbols:

\[
\min
\sum_s w_s\left[
\sum_i \left(c_i^{fixed}y_i+c_i^{regular}q_i+c_i^{capital}q_i\right)
+ \sum_{i,t}
\left(c_i^{hold}I_{its}+c_i^{stockout}b_{its}+c_i^{expedite}x_{its}\right)
\right].
\]

Working capital is charged on ordered units in the frozen smoke contract. It belongs to the
objective but not the hard purchase budget. The objective contains no transfer term because
transfers are deferred.

## 8. Named scenario construction

- `normal` draws q10/q50/q90 from the adapter with configured basis-point weights
  2000/6000/2000 and uses the governed lead time.
- `demand_surge` applies the frozen 135% integer transformation to the keyed marginal draw while
  retaining normal lead time.
- `supplier_delay` reuses the exact `normal` demand draw for the same seed and adds three days to
  regular lead time.

The exact quantile selections and transformations belong to the versioned configuration. These
three paths are useful stress cases; they are not samples from a proven joint distribution.

## 9. Heuristic control and fallback

The deterministic heuristic calculates a base-stock/reorder-point target for each line, then
applies the frozen lot-sizing and shared-resource allocation rules. It is both:

- the comparison policy; and
- the candidate fallback after solver `UNKNOWN`, and a diagnostic plan after a controlled error.

The heuristic receives no exemption from hard constraints. Its persisted trajectory is passed to
the same independent validator. Only raw `UNKNOWN` plus full independent feasibility, including
service, may become `REVIEWABLE`; error and model-invalid paths remain receipt-only even if the
diagnostic heuristic is feasible.

## 10. Outcome semantics

The raw solver status, independent feasibility, and review disposition are separate fields.

- Raw `OPTIMAL` means CP-SAT proved optimality for its encoded model, not that the business
  contract was independently validated.
- Raw `FEASIBLE` means CP-SAT found an incumbent, not that optimality was proved.
- Raw `INFEASIBLE` maps to `RECEIPT_ONLY_INFEASIBLE`.
- Raw `UNKNOWN` triggers the deterministic heuristic and maps to `REVIEWABLE` only after full
  independent feasibility; otherwise it is receipt-only.
- Raw `ERROR` and `MODEL_INVALID` map to `RECEIPT_ONLY_SOLVER_FAILURE`, even when a diagnostic
  fallback is independently feasible.
- Invalid input, nonfinite/scaling failure, or tamper failure is receipt-only.

Only independent hard feasibility permits a recommendation manifest and `pending_approval` event.
R4A never creates an approval or execution event.

## 11. Proof obligations

The bounded gate must test:

1. exact inventory and demand conservation;
2. nonnegative integral quantities and every hard constraint;
3. candidate objective agreement with an exhaustive tiny-instance oracle;
4. feasibility monotonicity when a frozen hard bound is relaxed;
5. non-worsening optimal objective when a capacity is increased on a feasible tiny case;
6. deterministic replay and exact normalized cross-platform receipt comparison through
   `nexus r4a compare-platforms`;
7. reviewable `UNKNOWN` fallback when independently feasible and receipt-only controlled-error
   behavior;
8. receipt-only invalid/infeasible behavior; and
9. fingerprint rejection after any material mutation.

Evaluation regret is computed separately for every seed/scenario path against a
perfect-information CP-SAT solve that sees only that path. The policy cost-difference interval is a
95% deterministic bootstrap over whole seed clusters; it never resamples the three paths from one
seed independently.

Passing these obligations would prove bounded implementation mechanics only. It would not prove
that the model assumptions, costs, or scenario distribution match a real supply chain.
